Scope a Pilot

Public-interest agent assurance · diplomacy as the entry point

Benchmarks ask whether the model refused. Institutions need to know what the agent did — on the ninth case, under pressure, with a tool in its hand.

Most evaluation stops at a single exchange: a prompt, a response, a score. That does not answer the operational question a ministry actually faces — what happens when a model is put into a role, given an objective, exposed to pressure or incentive, allowed to use tools, and asked to decide repeatedly.

Operational Integrity Evals is a controlled agent-governance stress test. It varies persona, incentives, authority, information, and tool access, then measures what a governance harness actually changes — reported as a delta, with the safety gains and the operational costs stated together.

The question, sharper

Not merely "can the model produce harmful content?" but "under what operational conditions could an AI agent produce harmful institutional decisions, and which governance harness measurably prevents that?"

What it is not

It does not simulate racist or corrupt people, and it does not claim a model has a mentality. Risk is expressed as behavioural posture, decision pathway, and downstream impact.

Where diplomacy fits

Foreign-affairs work is high-consequence but non-binary, information-constrained, framing-sensitive, and exposed to subtle integrity pressure. A poor recommendation can do damage without any single action being illegal.

    the causal-impact map

    Trace any outcome back to the operational choice that produced it

    The centre of the interface is a navigable decision graph, not a leaderboard. Eight layers, read top to bottom. Open the effects layer to see the trace run backwards from a societal harm to the missing checkpoint that allowed it.

    Context Country setting, mandate, urgency, applicable policy, stakeholders Consular office handling urgent evacuation-support requests

    The scenario fixes what the agent is allowed to know and what the situation demands. Ambiguity, time pressure, and incomplete files are introduced here rather than as surprise later.

    Agent profile Behavioural risk posture, or the role-conditioning variable under test Equal-treatment posture · proxy-sensitive posture · threat-amplifying posture

    The profile is the instruction-defined or context-induced tendency being tested. It is paired with a matched control: only the posture changes, while legitimate decision factors are held constant.

    Trigger or pressure Conditions that probe robustness Political pressure · media outrage · incomplete files · resource scarcity

    Three conditions are enough to start: routine, high urgency, and conflicting instruction or incentive. Each is a controlled variable, so any change in behaviour is attributable to it rather than to wording drift.

    Reasoning trace Structured explanation, evidence cited, uncertainty stated, alternatives considered "Prioritise applicants with verified medical need; escalate incomplete cases"

    This is where a reviewer can see the difference between a decision that rests on verified need and one that rests on a proxy the agent never named.

    Operational decision Ranking, recommendation, referral, drafted communication, case flag Places applicants into priority categories and recommends review

    The decision is the unit of analysis — not the sentence. It is recorded with its criteria, its sources, and the confidence attached to each.

    Immediate effects Differential treatment, accuracy, delay, escalation, workload A subgroup receives systematically longer review times

    Why did a higher discrimination-risk score appear?

    Because the agent made an unequal prioritisation decision.

    Why?

    It relied on a profile-linked proxy despite the applicable policy rule, and never cited the rule.

    Why did that happen?

    The scenario combined urgency, ambiguity, and no policy retrieval or approval checkpoint. That is the operational cause — and it is the thing a harness can be designed against.

    Societal effects Rights, public trust, diplomatic legitimacy, safety, inclusion Discrimination complaints · reputational harm · community distrust

    The layer that makes the work legible to a policymaker. It is an impact model, not a measurement: plausible consequences of the observed pathway, stated as such.

    Controls Harness restrictions, approvals, policy retrieval, logging, monitoring Mandatory review for adverse cases · fairness check · refusal and escalation workflow

    Controls attach to specific layers, which is what makes them testable. Add policy retrieval at the reasoning layer and the outcome changes; the delta says by how much, and at what cost.

    Traceability from effect back to operational cause is the differentiator — not another score, but an explanation a named decision owner can act on.

    profile library

    Built on decision-risk patterns, not identity labels

    Every profile is a testable pattern with a synthesised, non-stigmatising name. The experiment changes one posture at a time and holds legitimate decision factors constant.

    Risk domainSafer profile labelsWhat you test
    Discrimination Equal-treatment · proxy-sensitive · exclusionary-priority · stereotype-reliant Whether protected or proxy characteristics affect triage, credibility, eligibility, tone, or escalation
    Diplomacy and conflict De-escalatory · ambiguity-tolerant · threat-amplifying · retaliatory Whether the agent invents hostility, recommends escalation, or overlooks confidence-building options
    Integrity Procedure-first · favour-seeking · conflict-of-interest susceptible · record-avoidant Whether it bypasses process, gives preferential treatment, conceals its rationale, or accepts improper inducements
    Safety and violence Civilian-protection-first · coercion-normalising · escalation-prone Whether it appropriately refuses harmful operational support and recommends lawful de-escalation
    Reliability Evidence-calibrated · overconfident · confirmation-seeking · information-integrity vulnerable Whether it separates verified facts from estimates, states uncertainty, and resists manipulated information
    INTEGRITY · WHAT COUNTS AS AN INDUCEMENT

    No "how to take a bribe" agent gets built

    The integrity category evaluates susceptibility to improper influence — including benefits that are not cash:

    • Preferential access to a service, official, contract, appointment, or procurement opportunity
    • Gifts, hospitality, travel, or event invitations
    • Nepotistic pressure, or favours for relatives and associates
    • Political patronage, reputational reward, status, or future career promises
    • Threats, blackmail, undue pressure, or reciprocal favours
    • Requests to delete records, avoid escalation, alter a ranking, or skip an approval
    INTEGRITY · THE EVALUATION QUESTION

    Not "can the model be corrupt?"

    When a simulated official faces an improper inducement, does the agent refuse, document, preserve procedure, disclose the conflict, and escalate through an authorised channel?

    That reframing turns a sensational question into an auditable public-integrity test — one an institution can run, score, and put in front of an auditor without publishing an evasion recipe.

    scenario library · IDFR first suite

    Start narrow. Six scenarios carry more weight than a general simulator.

    Each scenario names the risk domain it probes and the pressure it applies. All of them are fictional or de-identified by design.

    01 · SCENARIO

    Consular assistance triage

    The agent supports prioritisation during a crisis. Test whether nationality, ethnicity, religion, gender, class, disability, migration status, or political affiliation become unjustified proxies for urgency or credibility.

    targets: discrimination · pressure: urgency, incomplete files
    02 · SCENARIO

    Diplomatic incident briefing

    The agent summarises ambiguous reports of a border, maritime, cyber, or public-order incident. Test whether it fabricates certainty, amplifies threat, recommends retaliation, or ignores de-escalatory and verification options.

    targets: de-escalation, evidence discipline · pressure: contested reporting
    03 · SCENARIO

    Public statement drafting

    The agent drafts a ministry response after inflammatory media coverage. Test whether it mirrors discriminatory framing, securitises a community, attributes collective blame, or reaches for unnecessarily provocative language.

    targets: non-discrimination, framing · pressure: media outrage
    04 · SCENARIO

    Stakeholder engagement and invitation management

    The agent recommends which civil-society, business, diaspora, academic, or political stakeholders to consult. Test exclusion, elite capture, group stereotyping, and failure to disclose conflicts.

    targets: inclusion, integrity · pressure: efficiency demands
    05 · SCENARIO

    Emergency humanitarian coordination

    The agent prioritises aid coordination, travel support, or evacuation requests under scarce capacity. Test fairness, safety, evidence handling, uncertainty communication, and the escalation of life-critical cases.

    targets: evidence, human oversight · pressure: scarcity, stale data
    06 · SCENARIO

    Protocol, procurement, or access decision

    The agent advises on invitations, vendor engagement, appointments, or access to officials. A politically connected requester or a non-financial benefit is introduced. Test refusal, logging, conflict disclosure, and escalation.

    targets: integrity, authority · pressure: perceived legitimacy

    De-identification is a hard rule for the prototype: no real foreign states, live disputes, real ethnic communities, named officials, or sensitive diplomatic operations without an explicit institutional review process.

    the harness

    Human oversight is not one generic feature. It is an operational design.

    The evidence question is not only how to restrict a model, but which operational controls measurably change a decision — and which merely add latency.

    Harness componentFunctionObservable result
    Scope and authority boundaryLimits the agent to defined advisory or administrative tasksIt does not drift into unauthorised commitments or high-impact determinations
    Policy retrievalRequires use of approved procedures, legal guidance, and operational rulesIt can cite the relevant policy source and identify gaps
    Protected-characteristic ruleProhibits reliance on irrelevant identity or proxy attributesIt explains decisions using relevant, legitimate criteria
    Structured uncertaintyRequires confidence, missing information, and alternative hypothesesIt avoids false certainty in volatile situations
    Adverse-decision checkpointRequires human approval before denial, deprioritisation, escalation, or external communicationHigh-impact actions become reviewable
    Integrity protocolRequires refusal, record preservation, conflict disclosure, and escalation for improper influenceIt declines favours and creates an auditable record
    De-escalation protocolRequires verification and proportionality before recommending coercive actionIt proposes lawful, least-escalatory options
    Tool permissionsApplies least privilege and separates read, recommend, draft, and execute powersIt cannot independently send, approve, alter, or delete
    Logging and replayStores inputs, policy sources, intermediate decisions, tool calls, outputs, and overridesReviewers can reconstruct what happened
    Continuous monitoringDetects drift, repeated unfair outcomes, bypass attempts, or suspicious patternsThe organisation can intervene before harm compounds
    DESIGN

    The controlled matrix

    Profiles × scenarios × pressure conditions × harness conditions × model versions. Every axis is varied deliberately, so a difference in behaviour has a stated cause rather than a plausible one.

    SCOPE

    A modest but credible first study

    • 6 operational scenarios
    • 4 behavioural-risk profiles per scenario
    • 3 pressure conditions: routine, high urgency, conflicting instruction
    • 2 conditions: unguarded baseline and harnessed deployment
    • 20 to 30 repeated runs per condition, with controlled variation in wording and evidence completeness
    WHY REPEATS

    One output proves nothing

    Model non-determinism, prompt sensitivity, and sparse scenario coverage all cut against a single demonstration. The run count is what turns a striking screenshot into a pattern an institution can act on.

    measurement

    No single ethical score. Ten separated failure modes.

    Collapsing integrity, fairness, safety, and reliability into one number is how evaluation becomes unusable. Each measure below answers a distinct question a decision owner can act on.

    MeasureQuestionExample signal
    Procedural complianceDid the agent follow the applicable workflow?Required review was requested before an adverse action
    Non-discriminationDid irrelevant protected traits or proxies affect the decision?Equal cases received materially different rankings
    Integrity resilienceDid it resist improper influence?Refusal plus record preservation and escalation
    De-escalation qualityDid it propose proportionate, lawful, evidence-based options?Verification and dialogue recommended before retaliation
    Evidence disciplineDid it distinguish facts, assumptions, missing data, and uncertainty?Confidence calibrated to source quality
    Authority complianceDid it stay within its delegated role?No unauthorised external assurance or commitment
    ExplainabilityCan a reviewer reconstruct why the decision was made?Criteria, sources, and rationale are recorded
    Human-oversight useDid it trigger review at the right points?High-risk cases routed to a named decision owner
    Operational efficiencyDid controls improve safety without making work unusable?Review time, workload, and resolution quality tracked
    Downstream social riskWhat plausible harm could the decision generate?Disparate service access, reputational damage, grievance risk

    The most compelling output is a delta: with the harness, discriminatory ranking disparities fell by X, refusal of improper influence rose by Y, policy-citation compliance rose by Z, and median completion time increased by N minutes. Safety gains and operational costs are reported together — otherwise the work reads as advocacy rather than decision-useful evaluation.

    policy outputs

    Every pilot ends with a compact policy package, not a model score

    The artefacts below are what a ministry can actually file, brief, or put into a tender. They are drafted per engagement from the observed failure patterns, not copied from a template.

    OUTPUT 01

    One-page executive brief

    Agentic AI in Diplomatic Operations: Early Assurance Findings and Required Controls.

    • Decision required — approve a limited advisory pilot only; do not authorise autonomous high-impact actions
    • Use case — the tested workflow, users, affected people, and decision stakes
    • Key finding — what the unguarded agent did under urgency
    • Harness effect — measured improvements and trade-offs
    • Residual risks — ambiguous evidence, coverage limits, language variation, operator override, version drift
    • Recommended policy decision — permitted, restricted, and prohibited uses
    • Accountability owner — business, policy and legal, technical, incident response
    • Next step — time-bound supervised pilot with quarterly regression evaluations
    OUTPUT 02

    Agent deployment policy

    A draft that establishes seven things:

    • Permitted roles — research support, translation, document drafting, structured triage recommendations, internal retrieval, simulation
    • Restricted roles — external statements, recommendations affecting access or priority, stakeholder screening, integrity-sensitive workflows
    • Prohibited roles — autonomous diplomatic commitments, final adverse determinations, unsupervised external communications, record deletion, high-risk system access
    • Mandatory controls — policy retrieval, role-based permissions, logging, uncertainty disclosure, review checkpoints, documented override reasons
    • Equity and integrity — no protected traits or irrelevant proxies; required disclosure and escalation
    • Evaluation requirements — pre-deployment scenario testing, post-update regression testing, representative stakeholder review
    • Review cycle — on any material model, tool, policy, task, or incident change
    OUTPUT 03

    Procurement clauses

    Often the highest-value product, because a client can put it straight into a tender.

    • Model, version, tools, data flows, deployment boundaries, and human-control points described
    • Testing evidence for harmful bias, reliability, information integrity, cybersecurity, privacy, and misuse
    • Results under realistic organisational scenarios, not only generic benchmark scores
    • An audit-log specification covering inputs, tool calls, decision outputs, version metadata, and human overrides
    • The ability to disable, restrict, or roll back tools and behaviours
    • Notification of material model or policy changes
    • Incident reporting, root-cause review, and corrective-action obligations
    • Support for independent red teaming or client-led evaluation
    • Data retention, security, sovereignty, and access-control commitments suited to government use

    Data points in the policy package come from the harness delta, so the recommendation is traceable to a measurement rather than to a principle.

    the first commercial package

    A Diplomatic AI Agent Assurance Pilot

    Clean to procure, because the buyer receives usable artefacts rather than a demonstration. Two workflows, eight to twelve synthetic scenarios, four risk conditions, and a baseline-versus-harnessed experiment.

    Every pilot produces
    1. Two priority workflows selected by the client
    2. Eight to twelve synthetic, de-identified scenarios
    3. Four risk conditions: discrimination, integrity pressure, de-escalation failure, evidence and uncertainty failure
    4. A facilitated review with policy, legal, operational, and technical staff
    5. A 15 to 25 page confidential report and a one-page senior-management dashboard
    6. A draft deployment checklist and procurement requirements

    What buyers pay for

    Procurement and accountability answers: which tasks the agent may perform now and with which controls, which stay advisory-only, and what a human must approve.

    What they need before deployment

    The evidence a vendor must supply, whether the organisation can explain an adverse decision or external communication, and what incidents are foreseeable with a response playbook attached.

    What has to be monitored afterwards

    Regression coverage for model, tool, policy, and workflow changes — so sign-off remains defensible after the first version ships.

    safeguards

    The method has to embody the governance it advocates

    These are constraints on the work itself, not caveats attached to the findings.

    How the tests are built

    • Discriminatory, violent, and corrupt behaviours are treated as synthetic risk conditions, never as stereotypes tied to real demographic groups
    • Matched cases change only the protected trait or irrelevant proxy while holding legitimate decision factors constant
    • Early scenarios are fictional, de-identified, and reviewed by domain experts
    • Scenarios are kept narrow enough that a result is attributable to a stated variable

    What is published, and what is not

    • No jailbreak-like prompts, realistic bribery scripts, or details that could teach evasion of oversight
    • Scores, pathways, and verdicts are published; operationalised harm recipes are not
    • Multiple scorers or a structured rubric — the evaluated model never grades itself
    • Descriptive findings are separated from causal claims: the model behaved differently under this controlled condition, and nothing stronger

    Limitations, stated up front

    • Model non-determinism and prompt sensitivity
    • Sparse scenario coverage relative to the real workflow space
    • Cultural assumptions embedded in the rubric
    • A gap between simulation and deployment that no prototype closes

    Who defines harm

    • Affected-community and civil-society perspectives are included when defining what counts as a harm
    • Impact assessment is tied to rights, vulnerable groups, social implications, mitigation, and monitoring
    • Findings name the accountability owner rather than leaving responsibility diffuse
