Why this series

The safety burden transfers. This is the manual.

Local and open-weight models are spreading through ministries, banks, and missions because they promise sovereignty, cost, and control. The promise is real — and on your hardware there is no provider SOC, no automatic patches, and no safety card to lean on.

After five articles you will be able to: detect the false comforts that make local AI feel safer than it is (01), measure how quickly fine-tuning re-elicits harmful capabilities (02), red-team the deployed system — retrieval, agents, and tool access (03), run a reproducible pre-deployment eval battery with Inspect (04), and write a fine-tune disclosure profile while publishing harm metrics safely (05).

How to read this series

Evaluation, not exploitation.

The publishing rule: scores, not exploits

Every procedure in this series is evaluation, conducted on models you are authorised to test, on your own machines. Publish scores, curves, and verdicts — never attack recipes or fine-tune corpora that operationalise harm for others. Open benchmarks follow the same shape: scenario ID, benign anchor, probe set, scoring rubric, degradation curve.

Honesty notes

The "fine-tune disclosure profile" is this series' proposal, not an existing standard. Command syntax moves — versions are pinned and re-verified before publication. All tool and organisation claims were live-verified at drafting time (UK AI Security Institute / Inspect, garak, PyRIT, Ollama).
